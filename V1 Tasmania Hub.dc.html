Why Tasmania

An island that rewards slow looking

Wellington rises straight out of Hobart's back streets — twenty minutes of climbing and you've swapped café conversation for cloud. Ride it at 6am and you'll share the mountain with nobody but the currawongs.

Head east and the roads open out along a coastline that looks unfinished on purpose: orange lichen on granite, long straights, a tailwind you didn't do anything to earn. It's the kind of riding that makes you talk less and look more.

Inland, the Central Highlands are a different argument entirely — dolerite country, switchbacks cut into rock that's been cooling since the Jurassic, gravel that rewards patience over power. We send our gravel groups up here in the shoulder seasons, when the light comes in low.

Spring (Sep–Nov) and autumn (Mar–Apr) are Tasmania's best-kept secret: cool mornings, dry roads, none of summer's bushfire smoke. Every departure is capped at 12–15 riders and led by Beardy McBeard, one of cycling's best-known photographers and a local who still rides every route like he's seeing it for the first time. It's why our Tasmanian cycling tours — and the cycling holidays we build around them — keep filling a season ahead. Ride hard, then let the après begin.
