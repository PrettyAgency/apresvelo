Overview

Tasmania's whole hand, in five days

Tour de Tasmania is five days out of Hobart and back, and it doesn't waste any of them. You'll climb Mt Wellington's 17.7km ribbon of switchbacks, grind gravel through the Pipeline Track, and finish with a Strade Bianche-style dash of white gravel into a vineyard lunch that runs long on purpose.

It's the tour we point first-timers toward, because it shows Tasmania's whole hand: sealed roads and dirt, coastline and forest, a gallery afternoon at MONA and a welcome dinner that starts the moment you land. Beardy leads every departure himself, which means the stories are true and the café stops are non-negotiable.

Twelve to fifteen riders, one support vehicle, and five nights where the hardest decision is what to order after the ride.

Your ride captain

Beardy McBeard

Marcus Enno

Marcus 'Beardy' Enno has spent two decades photographing professional cycling around the world — and came home to Tasmania more convinced than ever that these are some of the best roads he's shot anywhere. These days he splits his time between a camera and a ride captain's radio, leading Après Vélo's Tasmania departures personally.

He knows every café, every gravel shortcut, and exactly which pinch on the Wellington climb to save your legs for. Ride with him for five days and you'll finish with better photos of yourself than you've ever taken, and a stranger's phone number you'll actually use.
